## 1. Definitions

### 1.1 Data-protection terms

"Applicable Data Protection Law" means every privacy, data-protection, and
data-security law that applies to Processor's Processing of Customer Personal
Data under the Agreement, including, where applicable:

- Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ("EU GDPR");
- the EU GDPR as incorporated into United Kingdom law, the UK Data Protection
  Act 2018, and applicable regulations (together, "UK Data Protection Law");
- Morocco Law No. 09-08 concerning the protection of individuals with regard
  to the Processing of personal data and applicable CNDP measures;
- the California Consumer Privacy Act, as amended by the California Privacy
  Rights Act, and its implementing regulations ("CCPA"); and
- other United States state privacy laws that impose controller/processor or
  business/service-provider contractual requirements.

"Controller," "Business," "Consumer," "Data Subject," "Personal Data,"
"Personal Information," "Process," "Processing," "Processor," "Sell,"
"Share," and "Supervisory Authority" have the meanings given by Applicable
Data Protection Law. "Controller" includes a "Business" and "Processor"
includes a "Service Provider" or "Contractor" where the relevant law uses
those terms.

"Customer Personal Data" means Personal Data contained in Customer Data that
Processor Processes on Customer's behalf to provide the Service. It does not
include Personal Data for which Processor independently determines the
purposes and essential means of Processing, such as Processor's own business
contact, account administration, security, billing, and legal-compliance data.
Processor's Privacy Policy governs that independent Controller Processing.

"Data Subject Request" means a request by or for a Data Subject to exercise a
right under Applicable Data Protection Law.

"Personal Data Breach" means a breach of security leading to the accidental or
unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of, or access
to Customer Personal Data Processed by Processor. It does not include an
unsuccessful attempt or event that does not compromise Customer Personal Data,
such as a blocked scan, unsuccessful login, or rejected request.

"Restricted Transfer" means a transfer of Customer Personal Data that
Applicable Data Protection Law permits only when an approved transfer
mechanism or derogation applies.

"Subprocessor" means a third party appointed by or for Processor to Process
Customer Personal Data on Customer's behalf. It does not include Processor
personnel, Customer, a Customer-controlled recipient, or a third party with
which Customer contracts directly and instructs Processor to interoperate.

### 1.2 Other defined terms

"Service" means the Support Agent service and related support, hosting,
managed operations, or dedicated-deployment services identified in the
Agreement or applicable Order Form.

Capitalized terms not defined in this DPA have the meanings given in the
Agreement.

## 2. Scope, precedence, and roles

### 2.1 Scope

This DPA applies only to Processor's Processing of Customer Personal Data under
the Agreement. Schedule 1 describes the subject matter, duration, nature,
purposes, Personal Data, and Data Subjects.

### 2.2 Roles

Customer is the Controller and Processor is the Processor of Customer Personal
Data. If Customer Processes Customer Personal Data for another Controller,
Customer appoints Processor as a Subprocessor and:

- references to Customer instructions include lawful instructions Customer
  receives from that Controller;
- Customer represents that the Controller has authorized Customer to appoint
  Processor; and
- Customer remains the sole point of contact for that Controller unless the
  parties agree otherwise in writing.

Each party acts as an independent Controller for Personal Data it Processes for
its own account and purposes.

### 2.3 Deployment models

The parties' roles do not change merely because infrastructure is dedicated.

- In shared SaaS and Processor-hosted dedicated deployments, Processor
  Processes Customer Personal Data in the systems it operates.
- In a Customer-hosted dedicated deployment, Customer operates the data plane.
  Processor Processes Customer Personal Data only to the extent Processor has
  authorized support access, performs managed operations, hosts a component,
  receives a support bundle, or receives telemetry containing Customer
  Personal Data.
- Schedule 5 or the Deployment Schedule allocates controls for a dedicated
  deployment. Processor is not responsible for infrastructure, accounts,
  providers, or security controls allocated to Customer.

### 2.4 Order of precedence

If documents conflict concerning Customer Personal Data, the following order
controls:

1. the applicable official transfer mechanism, but only for the Restricted
   Transfer it governs;
2. this DPA;
3. the Agreement; and
4. Processor documentation.

An Order Form may supplement this DPA but may not reduce a protection required
by Applicable Data Protection Law.

## 3. Customer instructions and responsibilities

### 3.1 Documented instructions

Processor will Process Customer Personal Data only on Customer's documented
instructions, unless Applicable Data Protection Law requires otherwise.
Documented instructions consist of:

- this DPA, the Agreement, Order Forms, and Deployment Schedule;
- Customer's configuration and authorized use of the Service;
- Customer's use of connectors, tools, permissions, approvals, and automatic
  actions;
- instructions submitted by authorized Customer users through the Service or
  agreed support channels; and
- other written instructions accepted by Processor.

Processor may Process Customer Personal Data to prevent or address fraud,
abuse, security threats, or technical problems, but only to the extent this is
compatible with Customer's instructions and Applicable Data Protection Law.

If a law requires Processor to Process Customer Personal Data contrary to
Customer's instructions, Processor will notify Customer before Processing
unless the law prohibits notice on important public-interest grounds.

### 3.2 Unlawful instructions

Processor will promptly inform Customer if Processor reasonably believes an
instruction violates Applicable Data Protection Law. Processor may suspend the
affected Processing until the parties resolve the issue. Processor is not
required to provide legal advice or independently determine Customer's lawful
basis for Processing.

### 3.3 Additional instructions

If Customer requests Processing materially outside the Service, Processor may
decline it or require a written scope, reasonable fees, and implementation
time. The parties will amend Schedule 1 when an accepted instruction materially
changes the Processing.

### 3.4 Customer responsibilities

Customer will:

- comply with its obligations under Applicable Data Protection Law;
- have a valid legal basis for the Processing and for each instruction;
- provide required notices and obtain required consents or authorizations;
- collect and disclose only Customer Personal Data that is adequate, relevant,
  and reasonably necessary for the configured support purposes;
- ensure its instructions, policies, knowledge content, connected accounts,
  and authorized actions are lawful and accurate;
- respond to Data Subject Requests and regulator inquiries as Controller;
- protect Customer-controlled credentials, endpoints, infrastructure, and
  user access; and
- not intentionally submit special-category, sensitive, criminal-offence,
  government-identifier, payment-card, health, or biometric data unless the
  parties have first documented the necessity, lawful basis, and additional
  safeguards.

Customer acknowledges that support messages are free-form and may contain
incidental sensitive information that neither party solicited. Customer will
instruct its personnel and customers not to submit unnecessary sensitive data.

## 4. Processing restrictions

### 4.1 Purpose limitation

Processor will Process Customer Personal Data only for the limited and
specified purposes in Schedule 1 or as otherwise permitted by Applicable Data
Protection Law. Processor will not retain, use, or disclose Customer Personal
Data for an unrelated commercial purpose or outside the direct business
relationship with Customer.

### 4.2 No sale, sharing, advertising, or cross-customer profiling

Processor will not:

- Sell or Share Customer Personal Data;
- use Customer Personal Data for cross-context behavioral advertising;
- combine Customer Personal Data with Personal Data received from another
  customer or collected from Processor's own interaction with a Data Subject,
  except where Applicable Data Protection Law expressly permits it and only as
  necessary to provide or secure the Service; or
- build a Data Subject or household profile for use in providing services to
  another customer.

### 4.3 AI and machine-learning use

Processor will not use Customer Personal Data, Shopify Merchant Data, Shopify
Customer Data, or data derived from them to create, train, fine-tune, or
improve a generalized AI or machine-learning model for Processor or another
person.

Sending the minimum ticket context needed to a configured LLM for inference to
provide the Service is permitted Processing, not permission to train a model.
Processor will not authorize an LLM Subprocessor to use Customer Personal Data
for model training. Where Customer directly selects an LLM provider, supplies
its own credentials, or controls that provider's terms and settings, Customer
is responsible for selecting a no-training and appropriate-retention
configuration and for obtaining any consent required by Shopify or law.

Any separate use for model development requires a written amendment that
identifies the data, purpose, model, retention, parties, and safeguards, plus
every consent or authorization required from Customer, Data Subjects, Shopify,
or another platform owner.

### 4.4 Deidentified and aggregated data

Processor may generate and use data that has been deidentified so that it
cannot reasonably be linked to Customer, a Data Subject, household, device,
store, mailbox, or workspace, but only if Applicable Data Protection Law and
the applicable platform terms permit it. Processor will maintain the data in
deidentified form, not attempt reidentification, and contractually require any
recipient to do the same. This clause does not permit AI-model training that
Section 4.3 prohibits.

## 5. Confidentiality and personnel

Processor will ensure that each person authorized to Process Customer Personal
Data:

- accesses it only as needed to perform assigned duties;
- is bound by written confidentiality obligations or an appropriate statutory
  duty of confidentiality;
- receives privacy and security training appropriate to their role; and
- Processes it only on Customer's instructions, unless law requires otherwise.

Processor will apply reasonable background-screening measures where lawful and
appropriate to a person's access and responsibilities. Processor remains
responsible for its personnel's compliance with this DPA.

## 6. Security

### 6.1 Security program

Taking into account the state of the art, implementation cost, nature, scope,
context, and purposes of Processing, and the risk to individuals, Processor
will implement and maintain appropriate technical and organizational measures
designed to protect Customer Personal Data against a Personal Data Breach.
Schedule 2 describes the current measures.

### 6.2 Changes to measures

Processor may update security measures to reflect changes in technology,
threats, providers, and the Service, provided that the overall protection of
Customer Personal Data is not materially reduced during an Order Form term.

### 6.3 Customer-controlled security

Customer is responsible for secure configuration and use of the Service,
including its users, roles, permissions, Automatic-action settings, connected
accounts, credentials, endpoints, and Customer-hosted systems. Processor's
security obligations do not excuse Customer's responsibilities or extend to a
Customer-controlled system except as expressly stated in Schedule 5.

## 7. Subprocessors

### 7.1 General authorization

Customer gives Processor general written authorization to use the
Subprocessors listed in Schedule 3 and at **[SUBPROCESSOR LIST URL]** for the
Processing described in this DPA.

### 7.2 Subprocessor obligations

Before a Subprocessor Processes Customer Personal Data, Processor will enter a
written agreement requiring data-protection obligations that are no less
protective in substance than those applicable to Processor under this DPA,
to the extent relevant to the Subprocessor's services. Processor will conduct
reasonable diligence concerning the Subprocessor's ability to protect the
data. Processor remains responsible to Customer for the Subprocessor's
performance of those obligations as required by Applicable Data Protection
Law.

### 7.3 Changes and notice

Processor will give Customer at least **[30] days'** advance notice before a
new Subprocessor begins Processing Customer Personal Data, normally through
email, an in-product notice, or the public subprocessor page. Notice will
identify the Subprocessor and its service, location, and anticipated
Processing.

When an urgent change is reasonably necessary to address a security incident,
provider discontinuation, or legal requirement, Processor may give notice as
soon as reasonably practicable instead of in advance.

### 7.4 Objections

Customer may object during the notice period on reasonable, documented
data-protection grounds. The parties will work in good faith to address the
objection, which may include limiting data, applying additional safeguards,
using a commercially reasonable alternative, or allowing Customer to stop the
affected optional feature. If no reasonable solution is available, either
party may terminate the affected Service without penalty for future unused
service, but Customer remains responsible for fees accrued before termination.
An objection does not require Processor to provide a feature without the
Subprocessor needed to operate it.

### 7.5 Customer-selected providers and recipients

A provider is not Processor's Subprocessor to the extent:

- Customer contracts directly with it, supplies Customer-controlled
  credentials, and instructs Processor to send data to it;
- it receives data from Processor solely as a Customer-selected independent
  recipient; or
- it is Customer's own infrastructure, identity provider, mail provider,
  Shopify application, LLM account, storage, monitoring, or other data-plane
  provider in a Customer-hosted deployment.

Customer is responsible for appointing and instructing such providers and for
their terms and transfer mechanisms. Processor remains responsible for
transmitting only the data reasonably necessary for the configured integration
and for protecting the data while it is in Processor-controlled systems.

A fulfillment partner chosen by Customer and contacted through the
fulfillment portal or a Customer-connected spreadsheet is a
Customer-authorized recipient, not Processor's Subprocessor.

## 8. Data Subject Requests

### 8.1 Requests received by Processor

If Processor receives a Data Subject Request concerning Customer Personal Data
directly from a Data Subject, Processor will, unless prohibited by law:

- promptly notify Customer;
- not respond substantively except on Customer's documented instructions; and
- direct the Data Subject to Customer where appropriate.

Processor may respond without Customer's instruction only to confirm receipt,
verify that the request concerns Customer, explain that Processor acts for
Customer, or comply with a legal requirement.

### 8.2 Assistance

Taking into account the nature of Processing and information available,
Processor will provide appropriate technical and organizational assistance to
help Customer respond to lawful requests for access, information, correction,
deletion, restriction, objection, portability, opt-out, or appeal.

Customer is responsible for verifying the requester's identity, determining
whether a right applies, communicating with the requester, and giving
Processor clear instructions. Processor may charge reasonable fees for
unusually burdensome assistance that is not required because of Processor's
breach, after giving Customer advance notice.

## 9. Government and third-party demands

Unless prohibited by law, Processor will notify Customer of a legally binding
demand by a court, government, regulator, or law-enforcement body for Customer
Personal Data. Processor will reasonably:

- direct the requester to Customer where lawful;
- review the demand for facial validity;
- challenge or narrow an unlawful, overbroad, or disproportionate demand where
  there are reasonable grounds to do so; and
- disclose only the minimum data legally required.

Processor will document requests and disclosures as required by law. Nothing
in this section requires Processor to violate law or create a conflict of
interest.

## 10. Personal Data Breaches

### 10.1 Notice

Processor will notify Customer without undue delay after becoming aware of a
Personal Data Breach affecting Customer Personal Data. Notice will be sent to
**[CUSTOMER SECURITY CONTACT OR ACCOUNT OWNER]** using the contact information
available to Processor.

### 10.2 Information and cooperation

To the extent known and applicable, Processor's notice will describe:

- the nature of the Personal Data Breach;
- affected data categories and approximate Data Subject and record counts;
- likely consequences;
- measures taken or proposed to contain, investigate, and remediate it; and
- a contact for follow-up.

Processor may provide information in phases as the investigation progresses.
Processor will take reasonable steps to contain and remediate the breach and
will reasonably assist Customer with its assessment, regulator notice, and
Data Subject communication obligations.

### 10.3 Responsibility for notices

Customer determines whether to notify a Supervisory Authority, Data Subject,
merchant, Shopify, or other person, except where law requires Processor to
notify directly. Processor will not name Customer in a public notice without
Customer's approval unless law requires it. Notification under this section is
not an admission of fault or liability.

## 11. Compliance assistance

Taking into account the nature of Processing and information available,
Processor will reasonably assist Customer with:

- security-of-processing obligations;
- Personal Data Breach assessment and notification;
- data-protection impact assessments; and
- prior consultation with a Supervisory Authority.

Customer will provide the context and decisions needed for the assessment.
Processor may charge reasonable fees for substantial assistance not caused by
Processor's breach, after advance notice.

Processor will notify Customer if Processor determines it can no longer meet a
material obligation under Applicable Data Protection Law concerning Customer
Personal Data. Customer may take reasonable and appropriate steps to stop and
remediate unauthorized Processing.

## 12. Demonstrating compliance and audits

### 12.1 Information

Processor will make available information reasonably necessary to demonstrate
compliance with this DPA. Processor may first satisfy a request using current
third-party audit reports, certifications, penetration-test summaries,
security documentation, and written responses, if available and sufficient.
Processor is not represented as holding a certification unless expressly
stated in the Agreement.

### 12.2 Customer audits

If the information under Section 12.1 is insufficient, Customer may conduct an
audit itself or through an independent auditor that is not Processor's
competitor and is bound by confidentiality. Unless a Personal Data Breach,
regulator instruction, or reasonable evidence of material noncompliance
justifies otherwise:

- Customer may conduct one audit in any 12-month period;
- Customer will give at least 30 days' advance written notice;
- the parties will agree the scope, timing, duration, evidence handling, and
  security controls;
- the audit will occur during normal business hours without unreasonably
  disrupting Processor or compromising another customer's data or security;
  and
- Customer will bear its costs and Processor's reasonable incremental costs.

Processor will address a verified material deficiency within a reasonable time
based on its risk and complexity. Customer may share an audit report with its
Supervisory Authority, but otherwise will treat it as Processor Confidential
Information.

### 12.3 Regulatory audits

Processor will cooperate with a competent Supervisory Authority as required by
Applicable Data Protection Law. Nothing in this DPA limits a regulator's
statutory audit or investigative powers.

## 14. International transfers

### 14.1 General obligations

Each party will comply with Applicable Data Protection Law governing
Restricted Transfers. Customer's configuration of a processing location,
Subprocessor, or Customer-selected provider constitutes an instruction to make
the associated transfer, but does not replace a required transfer mechanism or
risk assessment.

Processor will provide information reasonably needed for Customer's transfer
impact or risk assessment. Each party will implement supplementary safeguards
reasonably necessary for the transfer it controls.

### 14.2 EEA transfers

For a Restricted Transfer of Customer Personal Data subject to the EU GDPR from
Customer in the EEA or otherwise protected by the EU GDPR to Processor in a
country without an applicable adequacy decision, the parties will execute or
validly incorporate the unmodified Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by
European Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 ("EU SCCs").

- Module Two (Controller to Processor) applies where Customer is Controller.
- Module Three (Processor to Processor) applies where Customer is Processor.
- The docking clause applies.
- The general written authorization option for Subprocessors applies with the
  notice period in Section 7.3.
- The optional independent-dispute-resolution clause does not apply unless an
  Order Form states otherwise.
- Schedule 4 supplies the information for Annexes I through III of the EU SCCs.
- The Supervisory Authority, governing Member State law, and courts must be
  completed in Schedule 4 before execution.

The EU SCCs control if they conflict with this DPA concerning the Restricted
Transfer. The parties will not modify the EU SCCs except as they expressly
permit.

### 14.3 United Kingdom transfers

For a Restricted Transfer subject to UK Data Protection Law, the parties will
execute either the then-current UK International Data Transfer Agreement or
the then-current UK International Data Transfer Addendum to the EU SCCs issued
under the UK Data Protection Act 2018. Schedule 4 supplies the corresponding
tables, selections, and security information. The parties will complete a UK
transfer risk assessment and supplementary measures where required.

This DPA does not reproduce or alter the mandatory UK clauses. The official
instrument must be properly completed and made binding before the transfer
relies on it.

### 14.4 Switzerland and other jurisdictions

Where Swiss data-protection law permits use of the EU SCCs, the EU SCCs apply
with the adaptations required by the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection,
including references to the competent Swiss authority and Swiss law. For other
jurisdictions, the parties will execute a lawful transfer addendum or other
approved mechanism when required.

### 14.5 Morocco

If Morocco Law No. 09-08 applies, each party will complete the declarations,
authorizations, and CNDP procedures allocated to it. Customer will not instruct
and Processor will not knowingly initiate a transfer of Customer Personal Data
from Morocco to another country until the required CNDP transfer authorization
or other lawful basis is in place. The Agreement and this DPA constitute the
written processing terms between Customer as data controller and Processor as
subcontractor, but do not themselves replace a CNDP filing or authorization.

### 14.6 Government access and conflicting law

Processor represents that, as of execution and after reasonable review, it has
no reason to believe laws or practices applicable to it prevent fulfillment of
the selected transfer mechanism. Processor will notify Customer if this changes
and will cooperate on supplementary measures, suspension, or termination of

# Schedule 1 — Processing Details

## A. Subject matter

Provision of an AI-assisted customer-support operations platform that receives
and organizes support communications, retrieves relevant commerce and
fulfillment context, generates support-workflow suggestions, presents or
executes Customer-authorized actions, sends replies through Customer-connected
channels, supports fulfillment escalation, and maintains operational and audit
records.

For dedicated deployments, the subject matter also includes the hosting,
support access, managed operations, and telemetry expressly selected in the
Order Form or Deployment Schedule.

## B. Duration

For the Agreement term and afterward only until Customer Personal Data is
returned or deleted under Section 13, subject to legal-retention and backup
exceptions.

## C. Nature and operations

Collection, receipt, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation,
retrieval, consultation, matching, analysis, classification, generation of
drafts and recommendations, transmission to authorized providers or
recipients, restriction, redaction, export, deletion, and destruction.

Processing is continuous or event-driven according to Customer's use,
connected mailbox and commerce events, webhook delivery, scheduled connector
maintenance, worker queues, and Customer instructions.

## D. Limited and specified purposes

Processor Processes Customer Personal Data for these specific business
purposes:

1. authenticate authorized users and scope them to Customer workspaces;
2. receive and thread inbound support communications from Customer-connected
   mailboxes or conversation providers;
3. classify, route, prioritize, summarize, investigate, and manage support
   tickets;
4. identify the relevant merchant customer, order, fulfillment, shipment,
   product, policy, and conversation context;
5. send the minimum necessary context to Customer-configured LLM inference to
   produce classifications, summaries, recommendations, and reply drafts;
6. show proposed communications or business actions to authorized Customer
   users for approval where required;
7. execute Customer-authorized replies, refunds, discounts, cancellations,
   order or shipping-address changes, fulfillment tracking updates, and other
   enabled tools within configured permissions and limits;
8. deliver replies through the Customer's connected mailbox so provider
   history and conversation threading remain intact;
9. route Customer-authorized escalations and relevant ticket context to a
   Customer-selected fulfillment partner through the portal or Customer's
   connected spreadsheet;
10. look up and ingest carrier tracking events and produce support and delivery
    analytics for Customer's operation;

# Schedule 2 — Technical and Organizational Measures

The following measures apply to Processor-controlled systems. Customer-hosted
responsibilities are identified in Schedule 5.

## 1. Governance and personnel

- Access to production systems and Customer Personal Data is limited to
  authorized personnel with a legitimate need.
- Authorized personnel are bound by confidentiality and receive role-relevant
  privacy and security instruction.
- Production and test environments use separate data stores and credentials;
  production Customer Personal Data is not copied into test fixtures.
- Security responsibilities are assigned for incident coordination,
  credential rotation, access review, provider configuration, and recovery.

## 2. Identity and access control

- Merchant dashboard access uses an identity provider and authenticated
  sessions or verified access tokens.
- Role-based access controls gate merchant functionality, and tenant/workspace
  identifiers scope data access.
- Processor personnel use named access where supported, least privilege, and
  MFA for production administrative systems.
- Access is revoked when no longer required, and privileged credentials are
  stored outside source control.

## 3. Encryption and secrets

- Production HTTP traffic uses TLS.
- Connector tokens, API keys, and tenant LLM credentials are encrypted at the
  application layer with AES-256-GCM before database storage.
- Secret encryption keys are separated from ciphertext and supplied through
  deployment secret management.
- Processor-operated database, object-storage, and backup encryption at rest
  is enabled where supported and recorded in the Deployment Schedule.
- Credentials, authorization headers, and secret values are excluded from
  application responses and ordinary logs.

## 4. Tenant and application isolation

- Customer business records carry tenant/workspace scope and database access
  paths enforce that scope.
- Authorization checks prevent a user or fulfillment partner from obtaining
  another workspace's records.
- Connector credentials are resolved for the relevant tenant/workspace; a
  credential is not shared across workspaces merely because the deployment is
  dedicated.
- Customer Personal Data is not used to provide another customer's support
  workflow.

## 5. AI and action safety controls

- The model does not receive direct database or provider credentials.
- Typed tools limit the operations the agent can request.
- A permission service is consulted before every tool execution.
- Customer-facing replies and actions with financial or account effects
  require human approval by default unless Customer has expressly configured
  a narrower Automatic permission.
- Ticket-state guards, action limits, and idempotency controls reduce invalid
  transitions and duplicate external side effects.
- Tool calls, permission decisions, approvals, state changes, and results are
  recorded in the application audit trail as applicable.

## 6. Data minimization and lifecycle

- Commerce data is retrieved for support and shipment workflows rather than
  copied as an unrestricted merchant catalog.
- Connectors request and use scopes and protected fields needed for enabled
  functionality.
- Prompt context is limited to information selected for the relevant ticket
  and workflow.
- Retention sweeps archive and delete ticket-linked data according to Schedule
  1, and account/shop deletion removes tenant data from active systems.
- Orphaned uploads are subject to a separate deletion sweep.

## 7. Logging and monitoring

- Operational logs use request, event, job, ticket, deployment, and outcome
  identifiers where practicable instead of message or attachment bodies.
- Known Personal Data fields in audit details are redacted in production.
- Access and business actions are recorded in an audit ledger appropriate to
  the action.
- Logs are access-restricted and retained for **[INSERT PERIOD]**.
- OpenTelemetry or centralized log export is enabled only when configured;
  payload telemetry must be minimized and governed by the Deployment Schedule.

## 8. Secure development and change management

- Source changes are version controlled and reviewed through the repository's
  development workflow.
- Automated type checking and tests cover tenant isolation, permissions,
  credential encryption, state transitions, connector behavior, retention,
  and deletion paths.
- Dependencies and migrations are versioned; production releases use a
  documented migration path.
- Secrets and real environment files are excluded from source control.
- Material vulnerabilities are triaged and remediated according to risk and
  the applicable support/maintenance commitment.

## 9. Availability, backup, and recovery

- Processor-operated deployments use scheduled backups appropriate to the
  purchased deployment tier.
- Backup encryption, location, retention, restore testing, recovery targets,
  and responsible party are stated in the Order Form or Deployment Schedule.
- Queue retries, dead-letter handling, and idempotency controls support
  recovery from interrupted work.
- A backup does not expand the permitted retention or use of Customer Personal
  Data.

## 10. Incident response

- Processor maintains a process to receive, triage, contain, investigate,
  remediate, and document suspected security incidents.
- Compromised provider credentials can be revoked or rotated.
- Evidence and audit history are preserved as appropriate for investigation.
- Customer notification follows Section 10.
